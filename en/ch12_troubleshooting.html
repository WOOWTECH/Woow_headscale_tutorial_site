Chapter 12

Troubleshooting and maintenance

A device that will not connect, a node that is offline, DERP relays, updates and backups — work through them layer by layer, in order. By the end of this chapter, you'll stop gambling on "let's just restart it and see" and instead find the problem systematically and leave a record someone else can pick up.

Find evidence before you restart anything

When something goes wrong on the tailnet, the most common mistake is to gamble on "restart it" or "reinstall it", which washes away the clues. The right approach is to record the symptom first, work through the layers, and only act once you have evidence.

Chapter task: learn the layered troubleshooting process, and handle common problems — connections, offline nodes, DERP, updates and backups.

Work through the layers, outside in

LayerWhat to checkTypical signal
Device and clientTailscale client status, the networkOnly one device has the problem
Connection to the control serverWhether the device can reach HeadscaleStatus shows offline, cannot log in
Connections between nodesWhether a direct peer-to-peer connection was made, DERP relayping fails, latency is high

Narrow the scope first: asking "is it only this machine, or every node?" immediately pins the problem to "the device side" or "the control server".

Six steps to troubleshoot

  1. Record the symptom and the time

    Write down "when it happened, what you were doing, what error you saw". A timeline is the best clue you have.

  2. Confirm the scope

    Try another device, or ask someone else to try, and decide whether it is one device or the whole tailnet.

  3. Check the client status

    Run tailscale status on the device and see whether the node is online and its IP address looks right.

  4. Check the control server

    Use the Headscale commands to look at the node list, the policy and the routes, and find the first clear error.

  5. Make the smallest fix

    Change one variable at a time, and verify after each change. Do not touch several settings at once.

  6. Leave a record

    Write down "symptom → diagnosis → action → result", so it is easy to hand over later, or to compare against if the problem comes back.

Symptom quick reference

SymptomLikely causeFirst thing to do
The device shows as offlineThe client is not running, or cannot reach the control serverRestart the client, check the network and the connection to the control server
ping failsThe ACL is blocking it, the direct connection failed, or the node is offlineCheck the ACL, and see whether the traffic is going through a DERP relay
Latency is very highThe direct connection failed and the traffic goes through a DERP relayCheck the NAT at both ends and the DERP settings
A new device will not joinThe preauth key has expired or is no longer validGenerate a new key, check its expiry time
Connections fail after a policy changeAn ACL rule is wrong or too strictCheck the policy syntax and the rules
Something broke after an updateVersions are incompatible, or a setting changedRead the release notes, restore from backup

Updates and backups

Updates:

  • Read the release notes first, and watch for breaking changes between versions.
  • Back up before you update, and pick an off-peak window.
  • After the update, confirm one by one that the nodes can still connect and that the ACL and the routes still work.

Backups: the things a self-hosted control server cannot lose are settings like "the policy, the preauth keys, the nodes and the keys". Back them up on a schedule, so you can rebuild the tailnet if something happens to the server.

No backup, no update: "finding out it is broken only after the update" is the most painful case. A backup is the precondition for an update.

Troubleshooting FAQ

How do I stop a problem from coming back?
Keep the "symptom → diagnosis → action → result" record from last time. When it happens again, compare against it — that usually locates the problem quickly.
Can I restart the control server myself?
It depends on whether you have access to the server. Ordinary users do not have it, and should not restart the server on a whim; ask the admin to handle it, and record the symptom first.
What is a DERP relay, and do I have to have one?
When two devices cannot make a direct peer-to-peer connection, a DERP relay forwards the traffic for them. If they can connect directly, it is not used; it is the fallback for when the direct connection fails.
How do I report a problem to the admin?
Include "the time, what you did, the error message (redact anything sensitive), and the blast radius (just me, or everyone)". The more complete it is, the easier it is to handle.
The interface or the commands changed after an update?
Versions change; that is normal. Read the official release notes and docs first. This tutorial reflects the version current at the time of writing, and where they differ, the latest one wins.
